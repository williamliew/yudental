Yu Dental Laboratory
Website Recommendations

Content, design, navigation and SEO

Prepared for client review · Wellington, New Zealand

Following work on the Yu Dental Laboratory website, this document summarises recommendations on content, design, and site structure. The aim is to strengthen how the site works for visitors and search engines, while keeping your family story credible and prominent without it overshadowing the practical information people need before they book or refer.

The site already has a solid foundation: a clear split between patient and clinician audiences, online booking, and a professional structure. These points focus on sharpening that further.

Overall principle
Your family heritage is a genuine strength, but most first-time visitors scan for answers quickly: Can you fix my denture? Do you see patients directly? How long does it take? Do you work with my dentist? Are you registered? The site should lead with those answers, with the personal story supporting trust rather than replacing practical detail.

1. Navigation and page structure

What is working well

  • Splitting patients (denture clinic) and referring clinicians (dental lab) is the right approach for this industry.
  • Core pages match real decisions: services, referrals, booking, contact, and FAQ.

Recommendations

  • Rename “Treatment/ Dental Services” to something patients understand immediately, e.g. Denture services or Patient services. The current label reads like internal terminology.
  • Give clinicians a clearer path: a For dentists section linking to lab services, digital scans, turnaround times, courier, and referrals.
  • Consider adding Book online to the main navigation, or keeping it highly visible on mobile. Phone and online booking are primary conversion paths in denture care.
  • About and Team overlap in content. Suggested split: About = business story, facility, timeline; Team = credentials and roles. Trim duplicated bios so each page has one clear purpose.
  • The referrals page should align with how practices actually refer (secure form, downloadable PDF, email workflow, or portal link), not only a placeholder form.

Industry context (NZ/Australia): Leading dental labs and prosthetic clinics typically structure sites around: who you serve → what you make → how to refer or book → trust signals (credentials, digital workflow, turnaround). Yu Dental is close; navigation labels and the clinician hub need one more layer of clarity.

2. Content

Homepage

  • The main heading is currently the business name. Consider an outcome-led, location-specific line, e.g. “Dentures, relines and repairs in Wellington” or “Family dental laboratory and denture clinic, Te Aro”.
  • Keep the opening paragraph shorter: NZ-made quality, plus who you serve (patients and referring dentists).
  • Move deeper family narrative below the fold or onto the About page.

Team page

  • Allan’s qualifications are clear and scannable; that format works well.
  • Steven and Leanne’s bios are long and read generically. For the public site: lead with role and credentials, then 2–3 short paragraphs or a brief quote. Deeper family narrative can sit on About.

Placeholder content to resolve before launch

These can undermine trust if left live:

  • Stock photography for team, hero, and about sections (replace with real clinic, lab, and team photos where possible)
  • Contact page parking notes (“update with your exact arrangement”)
  • About page draft text (“add your own dates and memories when you are ready”)
  • Team page instructions about adding images

Content gaps competitors often cover

Audience What they search or ask Suggested site response
Patients (especially 65+) WINZ / SuperGold, fees, emergency repair Clarify what SuperGold benefit applies; FAQ on subsidies and repairs
Patients Do I need a referral? Already in FAQ; repeat near the Book button
Patients Full vs partial, implant options, materials Plain-language service summaries with links to detail
Dentists Digital scans, turnaround, courier Explicit turnaround ranges and nationwide shipping
Dentists Crown/bridge/implant scope Align wording with actual scope so clinicians know what you offer

Tone: Some phrases read as generic marketing (“weaving compassion”, “opening hearts and doors”). Your real differentiators are family lab + clinic, NZ-made work, long heritage, and strong qualifications. Plainer language will feel more trustworthy to older patients and clinical referrers.

3. Design and look and feel

Strengths

  • Navy and teal palette fits the sector: professional without feeling cold
  • Readable layout, phone number and booking in the header, helpful icons and process steps

Recommendations

  1. Photography is the highest-impact change: reception, clinic, lab bench, finished work (with consent), family at work.
  2. Long pages (About, Team): use pull quotes, timeline graphics, and credential highlights to break up text.
  3. Service icon grid: consider linking each tile to the matching section on the denture services page.
  4. CTA language: “Let’s chat” may feel informal; “Call us” or “Contact the clinic” may work better alongside Book online for your audience.
  5. Trust strip near footer or below hero, e.g. “Registered dental prosthetists · NZ-made · Wellington since 2000”.
  6. Halaxy booking embed: ensure it works well on mobile, with a clear phone option directly above the booking widget.

4. SEO (search and discoverability)

Current setup

  • Page titles and descriptions are in place
  • Basic local business structured data
  • NZ locale and a fast static site

Recommendations

  • Title tags: include service + location where relevant, e.g. “Denture repairs Wellington”, not only the business name
  • One clear keyword theme per page heading (aligned with the page title)
  • Expand FAQ with real questions: emergency repair, travel from surrounding areas, WINZ quotes, implant dentures, number of appointments, what to bring to first visit
  • Add FAQ structured data on the FAQ page
  • Sync Google Business Profile with name, address, phone, hours, and categories (e.g. dental prosthetist, dental laboratory)
  • Link service names in copy to the relevant service pages
  • If targeting Australian referrers: short note on courier, scan formats, and how to send a case

Local search terms worth using naturally in headings and FAQ:

  • dentures Wellington
  • denture repair Wellington
  • dental prosthetist Wellington
  • dental laboratory Wellington
  • reline dentures / partial dentures NZ

5. Industry direction (NZ / Australia)

Useful context when prioritising updates:

  1. Digital impressions (iTero, 3Shape, Sirona) are standard for lab referrals. Your scan logos are good; a short “how to send a case” section would match what many AU/NZ labs promote.
  2. Implant prosthetics (overdentures, bar-retained, All-on-4) are in high demand. Ensure public claims are accurate and clinically scoped.
  3. Sleep apnoea and bruxism appliances are a growing niche; short patient-facing explainers would help.
  4. In NZ, WINZ, SuperGold, and health insurers matter to patients. Even “fees vary; we will explain options at consultation” helps more than no guidance.
  5. Dental Council registration, NZIDT membership, and prosthetist credentials are trust signals clinicians expect. Surface them near referrals and team, not only in affiliations.

6. Suggested priority

Before or at launch

  • Replace placeholder images and contact/parking copy
  • Simplify navigation labels and patient vs clinician paths
  • Shorten public bios; keep credentials prominent
  • Expand FAQ with real patient and dentist questions
  • Align homepage heading and page titles with location + services

Next phase

  • Real referral workflow for dentists
  • More detail on services (timelines, subsidy pathways, fees guidance)
  • Google Business Profile and FAQ structured data
  • Testimonials or case examples (with permission)

Ongoing

  • Blog or guides are optional if FAQ and service pages are strong

In summary: The website should feel like a capable Wellington family lab that patients can book and dentists can refer to, with your personal story supporting that message rather than replacing the practical information people need to choose you.
